Sparse in Time, Rich in Guidance: Higher-Order Temporal Modeling for Balanced Training-Free Music Editing

Abstract

Training-free music editing aims to introduce a target style while preserving the musical identity of the source. Existing methods either update the editing direction progressively or combine observations from a few flow times using predefined rules, without explicitly modeling the relationships among nearby observations. We introduce a delta-time perspective that treats these observations as estimates of the same source-to-target displacement at nearby flow times. Based on their temporal differences, we derive higher-order temporal guidance using a second-order Taylor approximation, capturing both the local trend and higher-order variation of the editing direction with analytically determined coefficients. We further select the expansion time through Bayesian optimization and apply the resulting direction in a single training-free and inversion-free update. Experiments on genre and instrument editing demonstrate improved target transformation, source preservation, and audio quality over representative baselines.

Audio Editing Results

We present qualitative results for instrument and genre editing. Each example compares the source audio with our edited output and covers one directed transition in the corresponding benchmark.
