In Tune with Time: Higher-Order Temporal Modeling for Training-Free Music Style Transfer

Abstract

Training-free music style transfer aims to introduce a target style while preserving the musical identity of the source. Existing methods construct editing guidance through progressive latent updates or temporal aggregation. We investigate how local temporal variation can inform this construction. Our key insight is that, with the edited latent fixed, differences among nearby evaluations estimate the local trend and curvature of the source-to-target displacement. Based on this insight, we introduce an efficient delta-time formulation that constructs higher-order guidance from three neighboring evaluations using a second-order Taylor model. The model combines the central displacement with its estimated temporal components, yielding aggregation coefficients determined by second-order reconstruction conditions. We further select the expansion time through Bayesian optimization and apply the resulting guidance in a single training-free, inversion-free update. Experiments on genre and instrument editing with DiT- and U-Net-based backbones demonstrate improved style transformation, source preservation, and audio quality over representative baselines.

Audio Transfer Results

We present qualitative results for instrument and genre editing. Each example compares the source audio with our edited output and covers one directed transition in the corresponding benchmark.
